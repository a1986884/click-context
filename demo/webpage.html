FIELD NOTES · 01

A small change in perspective

Learning a language changes the way we notice the world. A familiar street can become a place full of new questions.

Take a closer look at an ordinary word. A bank can hold your savings, but the bank beside a river offers a different kind of shelter. Keep the surrounding sentence in view.

Understanding grows when we connect a new idea to something we already know.

Reading expressions in context

We took into account the cost of the trial. In contrast to the earlier study, the result was statistically significant. The researchers carried out a second experiment to rule out chance.

In terms of cost, the new method is more practical. The next trial will take place in June. The team will take advantage of the available equipment.

Reading a long research paragraph

Scientific papers often introduce several connected ideas in a single long paragraph, so it helps to read one sentence at a time while keeping the overall argument in view. A figure reference such as Fig. 2 belongs with the sentence that explains the evidence, and a value such as 3.14 should remain a single number rather than become a sentence boundary. Equations, for example Eq. (3), can connect an observation to the method that produced it, while a link to the earlier discussion lets the reader check an assumption without losing their place. The meaning of a technical term may become clearer only when the author describes the conditions of the experiment or compares the result with an alternative explanation. Reading the English sentence followed by its Chinese translation can reduce the effort of moving between the two languages, especially when the paragraph extends across many lines on a narrow screen. After checking the details, return to the complete paragraph and consider how each sentence contributes to the main conclusion.

Build a reading habit

  • Choose a short article that interests you.
    • Read the first paragraph without looking up every word.
    • Return to the words that matter most.
    Let curiosity guide your next question.
  • Save useful words with the sentences where you found them.
